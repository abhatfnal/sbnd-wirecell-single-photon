# NC-Delta -> N gamma in SBND with the frozen Wire-Cell chain: first-look summary

Date: 2026-11-11. Source of every number: `NCDELTA_FROZEN_WIRECELL_BASELINE.md`. **Characterisation, not an analysis result**: no optimisation, no retraining, no approved fiducial volume, no purity, no S/B, no significance.

**What is the analysis?** A search for NC Delta radiative decay (Delta -> N gamma) as a single photon with zero (1g0p) or one (1g1p) reconstructed proton. The baseline is the unmodified production fg1 Wire-Cell reconstruction with the inherited MicroBooNE-derived single-photon BDT chain, run on the official Gen2 `EX_ncdeltarad` signal. Characterisation conventions (not approved definitions): candidate policy `any`, truth denominator = provisional TPC box (12,427 events), 35 MeV truth proton threshold.

**Baseline efficiency.** 1,153 of 12,427 selected: **9.28 %** (68 % interval 9.02-9.55; policy `first`: 1,101, 8.86 %). By truth topology: 0p 8.12 %, 1p 10.69 %, >= 2p 8.24 %. Delta0 7.61 %, Delta+ 11.32 % (not a clean charge effect: Delta charge, proton origin and topology are entangled).

**Where the signal is lost** (fraction of the 12,427, exclusive): no candidate 8.8 %; no usable > 20 MeV photon shower **26.2 %**; `numu` **24.4 %**; `other` **17.9 %**; `ncpi0` **11.8 %**; `n_20br1` 1.3 %; `nue` 0. Each is mostly a reconstruction-state effect, not a photon-energy threshold: the shower loss is photon typing (muon / pion / proton 30 % of it), shower attachment and fragmentation; `numu` rejects compact, few-segment photon objects (29 % survival under 20 cm of shower vs 74 % above); `other` follows vertex anchoring; `ncpi0` is a shower pair formed for a single photon.

**How well are 0p and 1p reconstructed?** Of the selected events 425 / 471 / 39 of truth 0p / 1p / >= 2p land in the matching reco channel (reco 1g0p 527, 1g1p 556, >= 2p 70). Truth 1p -> reco 0p comes mainly from protons that are mis-typed, merged into another object or have no row (not from the 35 MeV threshold); truth 0p -> reco 1p from the photon typed as a proton and neutron-induced secondary protons. Photon energy is reconstructed to 1.03 +/- 12.7 %; the Delta mass for true decay pairs to +2.8 MeV, 68 % [-44, +34]; the vertex is 0.9 cm from truth for 1p but 11.6 cm for 0p (displaced along the photon, decided before the final refinement).

**Dominant reconstruction limitations.** (1) The photon is mis-typed (27.6 % track-typed), fragmented (median 3 pieces) or owned by a hadron-typed object; (2) the vertex follows a proton when there is one and the photon when there is not, which drives `other`; (3) protons above 500 MeV are mis-typed muon / pion and below 50 MeV merge into other objects; (4) the pi0 and multiplicity logic splits single photons. Producer-side PID inputs (per-segment dQ/dx, template scores) are not stored, which limits the causal diagnosis.

**Backgrounds.** 635 selected non-signal BNB-CV events: 1g0p 310, 1g1p 230, >= 2p 95 (rates 1,140 / 846 / 349 per 1e20 POT, statistical only). 1g0p: NC pi0 33 %, outside-TPC neutrinos 24 %, nue CC 17 %; 1g1p: nue CC 37 %, NC pi0 30 %, CC pi0 18 %. The signal rate (40.6 per 1e20 POT in the TPC box) and this background are quoted independently; their ratio is not an S/B (no common volume, no beam-off normalisation). The natural NC-Delta in the BNB CV (13 selected of 117 in the box) agrees with the official sample within low statistics.

**Externally blocked.** G approved beam-off normalisation; H flash-time reference; J same-event SPINE output and exact SPINE efficiency definition. (Non-blocking: one missing merged-CAF event, 728 historical no-flatCAF units, `TotalGenEvents` bookkeeping.)

**Required before optimisation.** Record the analysis decisions on the fiducial volume, the proton threshold, the `included == 3` rows and the candidate policy (all open; decision support exists, no option is preferred for its efficiency); resolve G and H; obtain the SPINE output for a same-event comparison; then optimisation / retraining (L) can be designed against a defined denominator and a normalised background.
